The Launch Playbook

Ship your first profitable product in 30 days.

No audience required. No marketing degree required. Thirty days of "do this today," plus the templates that remove every blank page between you and your first sale.

How to use this. Don't read it cover to cover and feel productive. Open it once a day, do that day's task, close it. The whole method is: small action, every day, in public. If you only have 30 minutes a day, you still finish on time.

1 · The 30-Day Launch Map

One focused task per day. Weekends are deliberately light — keep momentum, don't burn out.

Week 1 — Decide what you're selling

Day 1List 5 problems you've personally paid to solve or wished you could. These are your candidate ideas.
Day 2Run each idea through the Idea → Offer Worksheet (Section 2). Kill the weak ones.
Day 3Pick ONE. Write a single sentence: "I help [who] go from [pain] to [outcome] with [thing]."
Day 4Find 5 real people who have this problem. DM them the question script (Section 5, "Validation DM"). Send all 5.
Day 5Read the replies. If 3+ confirm the pain in their own words, proceed. If not, return to Day 3 with what you learned.
Day 6–7Outline the smallest version of the product that solves the core problem. One outcome, done well. Nothing else.

Week 2 — Build the smallest thing that works

Day 8–11Build the core. Set a daily timer. "Done and useful" beats "complete and unshipped." Cut scope before you cut the date.
Day 12Use it yourself as if you were the customer. Write down every point of friction.
Day 13Fix only the friction that blocks the core outcome. Ignore the rest — list it as "v2".
Day 14Give it free to 2 of the people from Day 4. Ask: "What would stop you paying for this?" Listen, don't defend.

Week 3 — Set the price and build the page

Day 15Run the Pricing Calculator (Section 4). Write down the number. Add 20%. That's your launch price.
Day 16–18Fill in the Copy-Paste Sales Page (Section 3), section by section. One section per sitting.
Day 19Set up the checkout (a Stripe Payment Link is enough). Test a real $1 purchase, then refund yourself.
Day 20Build a 7-line "free taste" — a sample chapter, a mini-tool, a checklist — to capture people who aren't ready yet.
Day 21Run the full Launch-Day Checklist (Section 6) as a dry run. Fix what's red.

Week 4 — Get your first 100 customers

Day 22Make a list of 100 specific people/places where your buyer already hangs out. Be specific: not "Reddit", but r/specificsub.
Day 23Soft launch: tell the people who already replied to you. Use the "Warm Launch" script (Section 5).
Day 24Write one genuinely useful post about the problem (not the product). Publish where your buyers are.
Day 25Send 10 personal outreach messages using the "Cold-but-Kind" script (Section 5). Personalised, not blasted.
Day 26Public launch. Post the announcement. Pin it. Reply to every single comment within the hour.
Day 27Send 10 more outreach messages. Follow up (once) with anyone who showed interest but didn't buy.
Day 28Ask every buyer one question: "What almost stopped you buying?" Their answer is your next headline.
Day 29Rewrite your sales page headline and first paragraph using buyers' exact words.
Day 30Tally revenue. Decide: double down, adjust the offer, or take the lesson and start the next one. You shipped. That's the skill.

2 · Idea → Offer Worksheet

Run every candidate idea through these six prompts. An idea that can't answer 4–6 well is not ready to build.

1. Who, exactly? Name a specific person, not a demographic. ("Freelance designers who just went full-time", not "creatives".)

2. What's the painful, recurring moment? When does this hurt? How often? Frequent + painful = sellable.

3. What do they do today instead? If the answer is "nothing", the pain isn't real enough. You need an existing, inferior workaround.

4. What's the one outcome they'd pay for? Not features. The after-state. ("Sleep through a deploy" > "monitoring dashboard".)

5. Why you, why now? Unfair advantage = lived experience, access, or speed. One sentence.

6. Smallest sellable version? What could you ship in 7 days that delivers the outcome in #4, even imperfectly?

If #3 is "nothing" or #4 is a feature list, stop. Pick another idea. This step saves you the 29 wasted days.

3 · Copy-Paste Sales Page

This is the exact structure of a page that converts. Fill each block in order. Replace every [bracket]. Keep it in this sequence — it mirrors how a stranger decides to trust you.

[HEADLINE] The one outcome, stated plainly. → "[Get specific result] in [timeframe] — even if [their main objection]." [SUBHEAD] Who it's for + what it is, one sentence. [THE PROBLEM] Describe their painful moment in their words. Three sentences. They should think "this person gets it." [THE TURN] "It's not that you're [lazy/dumb]. You're missing [the real gap]." [WHAT IT IS] Name the product. One line on format + how fast they get value. [WHAT'S INSIDE] 3–6 bullets. Each = a component → the outcome it unlocks. [PROOF] A result, a testimonial, or a transparent "I built this because I needed it." [PRICE] Anchor (compare-at) → price → "one-time, instant access". [GUARANTEE] Remove the risk. "30 days, full refund, keep the files." [CTA] Action verb + outcome. Repeat it 3× down the page. [FAQ] Answer the 4 objections that actually stop the sale (price, fit, format, trust).

5 · First 100 Customers Scripts

Word-for-word. The rule behind all of them: be specific, be brief, make it about them, ask one small thing.

Validation DM (Day 4)

Hey [name] — quick one, no pitch. I noticed you [specific thing they did/said]. I keep hitting [the problem] myself and I'm wondering if it's just me. How do you currently deal with it? Genuinely just curious — trying to figure out if it's worth solving.

Warm Launch (Day 23)

Hey [name] — you helped me think through [problem] a few weeks back. I went and built the thing. It's done. Because you were early, here's the link before anyone else: [link]. No pressure at all — if it's not for you, a one-line "why not" would help me more than a sale.

Cold-but-Kind Outreach (Day 25 / 27)

Hi [name] — saw your post about [specific thing]. Not selling you anything: here's a [tip/resource] that helped me with the same thing → [link to the free taste]. If it's useful and you ever want the full version, it's there too. Either way, good luck with [their project].

Public Launch Post (Day 26)

I spent 30 days building [product] because I was tired of [problem]. It does one thing: [the single outcome]. It's [$price], one-time, 30-day refund. If you've ever [relatable pain], it's for you. Built it in public — happy to answer anything below. [link]

The "almost didn't buy" follow-up (Day 28)

Thanks for buying [product] 🙏 One question that genuinely shapes what I build next: what almost stopped you from buying? Brutal honesty > politeness here.

6 · Launch-Day Checklist

Run this top to bottom before you announce. One red item can sink a launch.

  • Checkout works — you completed a real purchase and refunded it
  • Success page loads and tells the buyer exactly what happens next
  • The product is actually delivered (download link / receipt / email) and you tested it from a fresh account
  • Sales page headline uses the buyer's words, not yours
  • Price, guarantee, and "one-time" are visible without scrolling on mobile
  • The free taste captures an email and the email actually arrives
  • Refund policy is written, honest, and you're prepared to honour it instantly
  • Mobile layout checked on a real phone, not just a resized window
  • Launch post drafted, link tested while logged out
  • You can answer "why should I trust you?" in one honest sentence
  • You've decided your "this worked" number before launch, so you can't move the goalposts after

One last thing

The people who succeed with this aren't the most talented. They're the ones who did Day 7's task on Day 7 instead of redesigning the logo. The map works if you walk it. See you on Day 30.
