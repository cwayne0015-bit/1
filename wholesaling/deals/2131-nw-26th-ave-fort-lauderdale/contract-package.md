## ⚠️ READ FIRST — CRITICAL GUARDRAILS (NOT LEGAL ADVICE)

> **This is not legal advice.** The two documents below are **draft starting-point templates only**. They have **not** been reviewed by counsel and must **not** be used, signed, or sent to a seller until a **licensed Florida real estate attorney** reviews and finalizes them for your specific deal.
>
> **Use the standard form in practice.** In real-world Florida transactions, the customary base document is the **FloridaRealtors/FloridaBar "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase (FAR/BAR "AS IS," current revision — commonly referenced as ASIS-7x)**, with assignment language added by addendum. That standard form is attorney-vetted, broadly accepted by title companies and sellers, and reduces the risk that your custom contract is rejected or unenforceable. **Treat the draft below as a learning/negotiation starting point, NOT a substitute for the standard FL form + attorney review.** Where practical, paste the assignment and contingency concepts below into an **addendum** to the FAR/BAR AS IS form rather than replacing it.
>
> **Wholesaling law is changing and varies.** Florida has tightened scrutiny of unlicensed real-estate activity. Marketing the *property* (rather than your *equitable/contractual interest*) can be treated as unlicensed brokerage. Disclosure rules, advertising rules, and assignment legality differ by jurisdiction and change over time. Confirm current Florida law with counsel before operating.
>
> **Be transparent — do not conceal the spread.** Disclose to the seller that you are an investor who intends to assign the contract for a fee. The documents include an explicit disclosure for this purpose. Disclosing assignment intent and the existence of an assignment fee on the closing statement is the safer, more defensible approach.
>
> **All [BRACKETED PLACEHOLDERS]** must be completed before use. Nothing here is filled in as final.

### 7. TITLE CONTINGENCY

a. Seller shall convey **good, marketable, and insurable title** by **[Statutory Warranty Deed / Special Warranty Deed]**, free of liens and encumbrances except permitted exceptions agreed in writing.

c. **Cure period.** If the title commitment discloses defects rendering title unmarketable or uninsurable, Buyer shall notify Seller in writing. Seller shall have **[ten (10)] calendar days** to cure such defects (with closing extended as needed).
d. **Buyer's termination right.** If Seller fails or is unable to cure within the cure period, Buyer may, at Buyer's option, (i) accept title as-is without price reduction, or (ii) **terminate and receive a full refund of the Deposit.**

### 8. PERMIT / CODE / LIEN CONTINGENCY

a. Buyer shall have the right during the Inspection Period (and through closing as to newly discovered items) to **review the permit history, building/code-enforcement records, open or expired permits, unpermitted additions or alterations, code violations, municipal liens, special assessments, and utility/lien-search results** for the Property.
b. Seller shall reasonably cooperate and provide documents in Seller's possession relating to permits, renovations, additions, and any notices of violation.
c. **Buyer's termination right.** If Buyer determines, in Buyer's reasonable discretion, that any open permit, unpermitted work, code violation, lien, or assessment is **material** to value, insurability, or resale/assignment, Buyer may **terminate and receive a full refund of the Deposit**, unless Seller agrees in writing to cure or credit such items at or before closing.
*(Relevant here given the ~1955 construction: open/expired permits and unpermitted additions are common risks.)*

## Quick how-to / negotiation notes (for you, not the seller)

- **Open at $100,000.** Keep your ~$115K ceiling out of every document and email — it appears nowhere above by design.
- **Low earnest money ($1,000)** plus a **15-day "any reason" inspection out** gives you a near-free option to control the property while you find an end buyer; that's the core wholesale mechanic. The narrower your contingencies, the stronger your offer looks — but here the four contingencies (inspection, title, permit/code, flood) are justified by the real risk flags (1955 build, suspected Zone AH, insurability/roof).
- **Assign via Document B** before closing; collect the fee through escrow, on the settlement statement.
- **Lead-based paint** disclosure is triggered (pre-1978) — don't skip it.
- **Best practice:** put the assignment + contingency language into an **addendum to the FAR/BAR "AS IS" (ASIS-7x)** form rather than using a fully custom contract, and have a **Florida real estate attorney** finalize both documents and confirm current wholesaling/disclosure/licensing rules before you send anything.
