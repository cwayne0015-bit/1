# South Florida Wholesaling — Lead-Generation Playbook
**Target market: Miami-Dade, Broward & Palm Beach (the tri-county area)**

> Scope note: This is a **deal-sourcing** playbook — finding motivated sellers and qualifying them. It does not run comps, set price, or draft contracts; qualified leads route to underwriting. All outreach must clear the compliance section (§7) *before* you dial, text, or mail.

---

## 1. Prioritized Lead-Source Channels (South Florida)

Ranked by signal strength (motivation per lead) blended with accessibility. Each channel notes the **specific** county source to pull from.

### Tier A — Highest-motivation distress signals

**1. Pre-foreclosure / Lis Pendens**
- *What it is:* A lis pendens is the public notice that a foreclosure suit was filed — the earliest reliable distress flag, months before the auction.
- *Who it reaches here:* Owners behind on mortgage, HOA, or condo dues. In SF this heavily overlaps with condo owners hit by special assessments and owners whose insurance premiums spiked.
- *Cost/effort:* Low cost, moderate effort (manual docket scraping or a $ subscription for cleaned lists).
- *SF data source:*
  - Miami-Dade: Clerk Mortgage Foreclosure Online System — https://www2.miami-dadeclerk.com/mfs/MortgageForeclosureSearch.aspx and the Foreclosure Registry https://bldgappl.miamidade.gov/foreclosureregistry/MainPage.aspx
  - Broward: Clerk case search https://www.browardclerk.org/Divisions/CircuitCivil (search recorded "Lis Pendens" in official records)
  - Palm Beach: https://www.mypalmbeachclerk.com/departments/courts/foreclosures (online auction calendar + case search)
- *Response characteristics:* Owners are stressed, guarded, often already pestered. Empathy and a fast, certain solution win. Best for door-knock + mail combo; phone if number is clean.

**2. Foreclosure auction calendar (last-chance window)**
- *What it is:* Cases set for online judicial sale. Owner is days/weeks from losing the house — strong motivation, narrow window.
- *Cost/effort:* Free to monitor; low effort.
- *SF data source (REALAUCTION-powered):*
  - Miami-Dade: https://www.miamidade.realforeclose.com
  - Broward: https://www.broward.realforeclose.com
  - Palm Beach: https://www.mypalmbeachclerk.clerkauction.com
- *Response characteristics:* Very time-sensitive; many already in denial. Lower close rate but high urgency on the ones who engage.

**3. Probate**
- *What it is:* Estates passing through court; heirs frequently want to liquidate inherited SF property fast, especially out-of-state heirs of snowbird parents.
- *Cost/effort:* Low cost, moderate effort.
- *SF data source:*
  - Miami-Dade Clerk online case search (OCS) https://www2.miamidadeclerk.gov/ocs/
  - Broward case search https://www.browardclerk.org/web2 (Probate division)
  - Palm Beach eCaseView https://appsgp.mypalmbeachclerk.com/ecaseview/
  - Note: inventories/accountings are confidential; case existence + personal representative are public.
- *Response characteristics:* Emotionally sensitive; heirs are often relieved to have an as-is buyer. Mail and gentle calls to the personal representative work well.

**4. Tax-delinquent / tax certificate & deed list**
- *What it is:* Owners delinquent on property taxes; published before the annual certificate sale (on/around June 1) and ongoing tax-deed pipeline.
- *Cost/effort:* Low cost, low-moderate effort.
- *SF data source:*
  - Miami-Dade Tax Collector — Delinquent taxes https://mdctaxcollector.gov/delinquent-taxes-current-year-and-prior-years and Tax Certificate Sales https://mdctaxcollector.gov/services/tax-certificate-sales
  - Broward Tax Collector certificate sale info https://browardtax.org/faqs/tax-certificate-sale/ ; legal-notice list https://browardcountylegalnotices.com/
  - Palm Beach Clerk Tax Deeds https://www.mypalmbeachclerk.com/departments/courts/tax-deeds
- *Response characteristics:* Often absentee or financially stretched owners; some are sitting on equity but no cash. Good mail targets.

**5. Code enforcement / code violations & liens**
- *What it is:* Properties with open violations (unsafe structures, unpermitted work, overgrowth, hurricane damage) — owner is tired, may be cash-strapped, often won't fix it.
- *Cost/effort:* Low-moderate; some county searches charge per-folio fees.
- *SF data source:*
  - Miami-Dade: Code Violations map https://gisweb.miamidade.gov/CodeViolations/ , Regulation Cases viewer https://www.miamidade.gov/Apps/RER/RegulationSupportWebViewer/ , Clerk Code Enforcement https://www2.miamidadeclerk.gov/cef/
  - Broward: Code Compliance case search https://dpepp.broward.org/BCS/Default.aspx?PossePresentation=SearchForEnforcement
  - Palm Beach: PZB fine/lien & open violation search https://discover.pbc.gov/pzb/administration/Pages/Search-Services-Administration.aspx
  - City-level matters too (e.g., City of Miami lien search https://www.miami.gov/Permits-Construction/Property-Information/Run-a-Lien-or-Violation-Search) — incorporated cities run their own code enforcement.
- *Response characteristics:* Owner is annoyed by fines; an as-is offer that makes the headache disappear resonates strongly.

### Tier B — High-equity / lifestyle-driven (SF niches)

**6. Absentee / snowbird owners (the signature SF list)**
- *What it is:* Owner mailing address ≠ property address. Huge in SF: out-of-state and international owners, seasonal snowbirds, tired long-distance landlords.
- *Cost/effort:* Low cost; the appraiser data is free, scrubbing is the work. Best ROI list to start with.
- *SF data source (free, owner mailing address included):*
  - Miami-Dade Property Appraiser https://apps.miamidadepa.gov/PropertySearch/ ; bulk data https://bbs.miamidade.gov/ (~$50/file); open data https://opendata.miamidade.gov/
  - Broward (BCPA) https://bcpa.net/ ; record-name search https://bcpa.net/recname.asp
  - Palm Beach (PAPA) https://pbcpao.gov/ (free, no login)
- *Response characteristics:* Lower per-lead motivation but high equity and volume; layer with tax-delinquent / out-of-state filters to find the motivated subset. Mail + SMS workhorses.

**7. Tired condo owners facing special assessments (post-Surfside, SF-specific)**
- *What it is:* SB 4-D / SB 154 require milestone inspections (at 30 yrs, or 25 yrs coastal) and Structural Integrity Reserve Studies; associations can no longer waive structural reserves. Owners in aging coastal condos are getting hammered with special assessments — real cases ran $134k/unit (Cricket Club, North Miami) up to $400k/unit (Mediterranean Village, Aventura). Many can't or won't pay.
- *Who it reaches:* Owners of pre-1995 condos in Miami Beach, Aventura, Hallandale, Sunny Isles, Hollywood, Boca, West Palm — especially absentee/snowbird unit owners.
- *Cost/effort:* Moderate; requires combining appraiser data (building age, condo flag) with association distress signals (assessment news, HOA-driven lis pendens).
- *SF data source:* Appraiser sites above (filter condos by year built ≤ ~1995, coastal ZIPs) + HOA-lien/foreclosure lis pendens in clerk records + local news on specific buildings.
- *Response characteristics:* Strong motivation when a five/six-figure assessment lands. Note for hand-off: condo deals carry assessment/reserve risk underwriting must price.

**8. Hurricane- / insurance-distressed owners**
- *What it is:* SF's soaring premiums, non-renewals, and storm damage push owners (and condo associations) to sell. Some properties are uninsurable until repaired; some owners simply can't carry the cost.
- *Cost/effort:* Moderate; no single clean list — assemble from signals.
- *SF data source / signals:* Code-violation/unsafe-structure cases (sources in #5), older roofs/build year via appraiser data, flood-zone overlays, post-storm damage areas, FPL/utility shutoffs anecdotally. Cross-reference flood zone via county GIS.
- *Response characteristics:* Owner is overwhelmed by carrying cost; "buy it as-is, you stop paying premiums/repairs" is the hook. Flag flood zone + insurability to underwriting.

### Tier C — Volume / supplemental

**9. Driving for dollars** — physically spot distressed exteriors (tarped roofs, overgrowth, boarded windows, code notices) in target ZIPs; confirm owner via appraiser sites. Low cash, high time. SF tip: target older coastal pockets and post-storm neighborhoods.

**10. Expired listings / FSBO** — sellers who already want out but couldn't sell retail (condition or association issues). Pull from MLS-expired services / Zillow FSBO. Disclose you're an investor, not an agent.

**11. PPC / SEO / "we buy houses" web leads** — inbound, pre-motivated, but competitive and pricey in SF. Reserve for moderate+ budgets.

## 2. Recommended Starter Channel Mix

**Low-budget (≈ $300–$800/mo, sweat-equity heavy)**
1. Absentee/snowbird list (free from county appraisers) + tax-delinquent + out-of-state-owner filter — your core mailing list.
2. Pre-foreclosure/lis pendens pulled manually from the three clerks (free).
3. Probate scrape (free clerk case search).
4. Driving for dollars in 2–3 target ZIPs.
- Outreach: 1 postcard/mo to a tight list (1,000–2,000 records) + manual cold calls/SMS to skip-traced, DNC-scrubbed numbers.

**Moderate-budget (≈ $2,000–$6,000/mo)**
1. Buy cleaned, stacked lists (PropStream/BatchLeads/REI tools) for absentee + high-equity + distress overlaps across all three counties.
2. Skip-trace + DNC scrub at scale; outsourced cold-call/SMS team or dialer.
3. Recurring direct-mail sequence (3-touch) to pre-foreclosure, probate, tax-delinquent, code-violation, and aging-condo lists.
4. Small PPC test for inbound.
- Layer the SF niches (special-assessment condos, insurance-distressed) as dedicated sub-campaigns with tailored copy.

Rule of thumb: start with the **free county-sourced absentee + distress overlap**, prove your conversion, then pay to scale the channels that already convert.

## 3. List-Building & Scrubbing Checklist

- [ ] **Pull raw lists** from the specific county sources above (appraiser for ownership/mailing/build-year/condo flag; clerk for lis pendens/probate; tax collector for delinquency; code portals for violations).
- [ ] **Standardize** addresses (USPS format) and parcel/folio IDs across counties (Miami-Dade uses folio; Palm Beach uses PCN; Broward uses parcel ID).
- [ ] **Dedupe** within and across lists by folio/parcel + owner name.
- [ ] **Stack/score** — flag records appearing on multiple lists (e.g., absentee + tax-delinquent + code violation). Stacked leads rank highest.
- [ ] **Equity filter** — estimate equity from assessed value vs. recorded mortgage/lien (clerk official records). Prioritize high-equity (room for a deal) and clear-distress.
- [ ] **Absentee/owner-occupancy filter** — owner mailing address ≠ situs address; flag out-of-state and international.
- [ ] **SF condo/SFR split** — tag property type; for condos capture build year + coastal ZIP for special-assessment targeting.
- [ ] **Flood-zone / coastal tag** via county GIS for insurance-risk notes.
- [ ] **Skip-trace** to get phones/emails (only for channels you'll call/text).
- [ ] **DNC scrub** every phone against the National DNC Registry + internal opt-out list **before** dialing/texting (see §7).
- [ ] **Suppress** prior opt-outs, do-not-contact, and already-worked leads.
- [ ] **Prioritize & assign** — top-stacked, high-equity, clean-number leads first.

## 4. Lead Qualification Rubric (call scoring sheet)

Score each factor 1–3. ~12+ and motivated = qualified → hand off.

| # | Factor | Ask on the call | 1 (weak) | 2 | 3 (strong) |
|---|--------|-----------------|----------|---|------------|
| 1 | **Motivation** | "What's making you think about selling, and how soon do you need this handled?" | Just curious / testing price | Some pressure | Clear pain — foreclosure, assessment, inherited, can't carry insurance |
| 2 | **Condition** | "What shape is it in — roof, AC, any storm/water damage, open permits or code issues?" | Turnkey (retail-minded) | Minor | Significant repairs / unpermitted / open violations |
| 3 | **Timeline** | "If we made a fair as-is offer, when would you want to close?" | No timeline | 60–90 days | ASAP / <30 days |
| 4 | **Price expectation** | "Do you have a number in mind?" *(capture it — don't commit to a price)* | Retail/unrealistic | Negotiable | Realistic / flexible |
| 5 | **Ownership & debt/equity** | "Are you on title? Any mortgage, HOA/condo assessment, tax or code liens?" | Title issues / underwater | Some debt | Free-and-clear or strong equity |

**South-Florida nuances to capture (don't score, but record for underwriting):**
- **Condo vs SFR**; if condo: building age, milestone-inspection status, any **special assessment** amount, monthly HOA, reserve health.
- **Insurance burden** — current premium, recent non-renewal, roof age, claims history.
- **Flood zone** — is it in one; flood insurance carried?
- **Hurricane damage** — open claims, tarped roof, unrepaired damage.
- **Occupancy** — owner-occupied, tenant-occupied (lease terms), or vacant/snowbird.
- **Out-of-state/international owner** — how they prefer to transact / sign remotely.

## 5. Outreach Templates

### A) Direct-mail postcard (empathetic, compliant, discloses investor intent)

> **Front:** *Thinking about selling your [CITY] property — as-is, no repairs, no agent fees?*
>
> **Back:**
> Hi [First Name],
> My name is [Your Name] with [Company]. **I'm a local real-estate investor** — not an agent — and I buy homes and condos in [Miami-Dade / Broward / Palm Beach] directly from owners.
> If your property at **[Property Address]** has become a burden — repairs, rising insurance, a condo special assessment, taxes, an inherited home, or just out-of-state to manage — I'd be glad to make a **no-obligation, as-is cash offer**. You pick the closing date.
> I can't promise a specific price until I learn about the property, but there's no cost and no pressure to talk.
> 📞 Call or text **[Number]** · ✉️ [Email]
> *If you'd prefer not to receive mail from us, reply STOP or call and we'll remove you right away.*

### B) SMS opener (compliant, discloses intent, easy opt-out)

> Hi [First Name], this is [Name] with [Company] — I'm a **local real-estate investor** (not an agent). I buy houses/condos as-is in [County]. Would you consider a no-obligation cash offer on [Street]? No pressure either way. Reply **STOP** to opt out.

*Texting rules:* only to numbers you've DNC-scrubbed and have a lawful basis to contact; honor STOP instantly; send during permitted hours; identify yourself and your business in the first message (see §7).

## 7. Compliance Note — handle BEFORE any outreach

**You (the operator) are responsible for clearing these before calling, texting, or emailing.**

- **TCPA (calls/texts):** SMS and many calls require proper consent or a lawful basis; autodialers and pre-recorded messages have strict rules. Honor opt-outs (STOP) immediately, call/text only within permitted hours, and identify yourself and your business. Maintain written records. Consult counsel on your dialer/SMS setup — TCPA penalties are per-message.
- **National & internal DNC:** Scrub every phone against the **National Do Not Call Registry** and your own internal do-not-contact list before each campaign. Re-scrub regularly. **Florida also has its own state DNC requirements and the Florida Telephone Solicitation Act (FTSA)** — Florida's mini-TCPA is aggressive on autodialed/automated texts and calls; get legal review for any automated calling/texting to FL numbers.
- **CAN-SPAM (email):** Accurate "from"/subject lines, identify the message as a solicitation, include a valid physical postal address, and provide a working opt-out honored promptly.
- **Honest disclosure:** Always state you're an **investor/wholesaler buying as-is — not a licensed agent and not a retail buyer.** Don't imply you'll list or represent the seller.
- **No price/close promises:** You qualify and route. Do not commit to a purchase price or a guaranteed closing — that's underwriting/contracts.
- **Public-records use:** County data is public under FL Ch. 119, but use it lawfully and respectfully; don't misrepresent how you got someone's info.
- **Respect & accuracy:** Be truthful with distressed sellers (pre-foreclosure, probate). No high-pressure or misleading tactics.

> This playbook is operational guidance, not legal advice. Florida's wholesaling, telemarketing (FTSA), and disclosure rules change — have a licensed FL real-estate attorney review your outreach and contracts before you run campaigns.
